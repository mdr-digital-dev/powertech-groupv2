Buzzers Are Dead. Why Chicago Businesses Are Switching to Video Intercom Systems

By PowerTech Group of Chicago  ·  September 2026  ·  6 min read

The standard commercial intercom — a buzzer panel by the door, a two-way speaker, and an entry button — has been the default for commercial buildings for decades. It tells you someone is at the door. It does not tell you who they are, what they want, or whether you should let them in. You release the door and hope for the best.

For most of that time, that was simply the tradeoff. Not anymore. Modern IP-based video intercom systems give you a live HD view of every entry point, full two-way audio and video from a smartphone app, a timestamped log of every visitor and door-release event, and direct integration with your access control and camera systems. The gap between what a legacy buzzer provides and what a current video intercom provides is enormous — and the cost difference has narrowed significantly.

For Chicago commercial buildings, professional offices, and multi-tenant facilities, the upgrade from buzzer to video intercom is one of the most practical security improvements a business can make.

What Video Intercom Actually Gives You

The core function is simple: when someone approaches an entry point, a camera captures a live HD feed that is delivered to a front desk monitor, a wall-mounted display unit, or a mobile app — or all three simultaneously. The person managing access sees the visitor before making a door-release decision. That alone is a meaningful change from a traditional buzzer, where the decision to release the door is made based on nothing more than a voice on a speaker.

Beyond that core function, current video intercom platforms offer:

  • Mobile app access — building managers, office administrators, or business owners can see and communicate with visitors and release doors from anywhere, not just from a fixed front desk station
  • Event logging — every call, every door release, and every failed entry attempt is time-stamped and stored, creating an audit trail that is searchable and exportable
  • Multiple entry points — a single platform manages all exterior doors, loading docks, parking garage entrances, and elevator lobbies under one interface
  • Pre-authorization — expected visitors can be pre-registered, so a delivery driver or scheduled contractor can be granted access via a QR code without live staff intervention
  • After-hours protocols — different access rules, notifications, and escalation paths can be configured for business hours versus off-hours entries, so the right person is always alerted

Integration with Access Control and Cameras

Video intercom is most effective when it is not a standalone product but part of a unified access control system. When the two are integrated, a door release from the intercom is logged as an access event in your access control platform — the same platform that tracks credential-based entries from keycards or mobile credentials. You get one complete record of everyone who entered through every door, whether they used a credential or were buzzed in by staff.

Integration with security cameras adds another layer. When an intercom call is initiated, the nearest camera view can automatically appear alongside the intercom feed, giving the person managing access both the close-up intercom camera view and broader contextual footage from the area outside the door. If an incident occurs later, you pull one timeline that shows the intercom interaction, the camera footage, and the door-release event together.

Key distinction: A video intercom records the moment of entry decision — who was at the door, what they looked like, and who released the lock. That record exists whether or not your cameras captured a useful angle. It is often the most valuable forensic evidence after a building security incident.

Multi-Tenant and Multi-Door Applications

The complexity of video intercom scales well for multi-tenant commercial buildings, which represent one of the most common use cases we encounter in the Chicago metro area. A single IP-based intercom system can serve dozens of tenants across a building, with each tenant receiving calls and managing their own visitors independently through the same platform. A visitor for Suite 210 calls up to Suite 210 directly — not through a building-wide intercom directory that exposes every tenant to every caller.

For buildings with multiple exterior entry points — a main lobby, a rear delivery entrance, a parking garage gate — video intercom provides unified management of all of them. The after-hours security team sees all entry points on one dashboard. Any door can be released from any authorized station or mobile device. No door is invisible to the system.

What Upgrading From a Legacy System Looks Like

The installation path depends on what is already in place. In many cases, existing door strikes and wiring can be reused, with IP-based intercom panels and door stations replacing the old hardware. In buildings with modern structured cabling, the intercom panels run over the same network infrastructure as other IP devices — cameras, access control readers, Wi-Fi access points. There is no separate dedicated wiring run required.

For older Chicago buildings with outdated wiring or no structured cabling, the installation involves more planning, but the result is the same: a modern system that can be managed remotely, updated over the air, and expanded without hardware replacements as the business grows or the building changes tenants.

The design work happens before installation begins. We map every entry point, determine which users and groups need access to which cameras and door releases, configure the mobile app permissions and notification logic, and test every scenario — including after-hours entries, delivery protocols, and emergency overrides — before the system goes live.

Who Benefits Most

Any business with a staffed or partially staffed front entrance benefits from video intercom. The businesses where the upgrade tends to deliver the most immediate value are:

Professional service firms — law offices, financial advisors, and medical practices that need to manage who enters their space, document visitor records, and prevent unescorted access to sensitive areas without putting the entire burden on a receptionist.

Multi-tenant office buildings where a single building management team needs to control access for multiple businesses without having each tenant manage their own separate system at the front door.

Warehouses and distribution facilities where a loading dock entrance is often unmanned but needs to be controlled and logged. A driver calls in, staff verifies them on camera, and releases the dock door without walking to the back of the building.

Businesses that have experienced tailgating — unauthorized individuals following credentialed employees through access-controlled doors — where video intercom at secondary doors provides a layer of verification that a keycard reader alone cannot.
