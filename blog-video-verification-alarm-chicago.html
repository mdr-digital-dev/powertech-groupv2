False Alarms Are Costing Chicago Businesses Money. Video Verification Fixes That.

By PowerTech Group of Chicago  ·  August 2026  ·  6 min read

If your business has a traditional burglar alarm, you already know the routine. The alarm trips — maybe someone forgot the code, maybe it was a sensor glitch, maybe the cleaning crew triggered a motion detector after hours — and a monitoring center calls to ask if everything is okay. You say yes. That is the end of it. No police, no investigation, no consequence.

Now flip the scenario. Someone actually breaks in. The alarm triggers. The monitoring center calls. Nobody answers. They dispatch police — if local ordinance even allows it without verification. Officers eventually arrive, check the perimeter, see nothing obvious from the outside, and leave. By then, whoever was inside is long gone.

This is the false alarm problem, and it is massive. Nationwide, an estimated 94 to 98 percent of all burglar alarm activations are false. Police departments in Chicago and the surrounding suburbs have responded by implementing false alarm ordinances with escalating fines and, in some cases, policies that deprioritize or refuse to respond to unverified alarms entirely. The practical result: the standard alarm system that most businesses rely on provides significantly less protection than owners assume.

Video verification changes that equation entirely.

What Video Alarm Verification Actually Is

Video verification integrates your security camera system directly with your alarm monitoring. When an alarm triggers, the monitoring center does not just make a phone call — a trained operator immediately pulls live or buffered video from the activated zone and visually confirms whether there is an actual threat in progress.

If the operator sees nothing — a spider on a sensor, a curtain moving near a vent, a forgotten employee — they cancel the dispatch request and log it as a false alarm. No police call, no fine, no wasted response. If the operator sees an actual intruder, they escalate immediately with confirmed visual evidence of a crime in progress. That changes everything about how police respond.

There are two primary approaches to video verification:

Passive video verification uses pre-alarm clip review — short video clips captured just before and during the alarm event. The operator reviews the footage and makes a dispatch decision based on what they see. This works well and is the most widely deployed form of video verification today.

Active video verification takes it further. Cameras with built-in analytics proactively detect motion and suspicious behavior and flag events to monitoring operators before an alarm even trips. Operators can watch in real time, issue audio warnings through on-site speakers, and contact law enforcement while an intruder is still on the property.

Key point: Video verification does not replace your alarm system — it makes it dramatically more effective. The hardware investment is usually modest when integrated with a properly designed camera system.

How Police Response Changes with Confirmed Alarms

This is where the business case for video verification becomes undeniable. When a monitoring center dispatches police with a confirmed alarm — meaning an operator has visually confirmed a crime in progress — law enforcement treats it differently.

Most police departments, including agencies throughout the Chicago metro area, prioritize confirmed intrusion calls significantly higher than unverified alarms. In some jurisdictions, unverified alarms may receive no response at all after a business accumulates false alarm violations. A confirmed alarm with visual evidence, on the other hand, typically receives a priority response.

The monitoring operator can also provide real-time intelligence to responding officers — number of individuals, their location in the building, whether they appear to be armed, which exit they are moving toward. That is a completely different level of response than "alarm sounded at this address."

For businesses with high-value inventory, sensitive data, or cash on premises, the difference between a 20-minute priority response and a 45-minute low-priority response can mean tens of thousands of dollars in losses prevented.

The False Alarm Fine Problem in Chicago and the Suburbs

Beyond police response, false alarms carry direct financial consequences that add up quickly.

Chicago's false alarm ordinance imposes fines that escalate with each subsequent false alarm within a calendar year. Many suburban municipalities have similar programs. Schaumburg, Arlington Heights, Naperville, and other collar communities have ordinances that can result in fees ranging from $50 for an initial offense to $500 or more for repeat false alarms — and in some cases, businesses that accumulate enough violations can lose their alarm response permit entirely.

Video verification virtually eliminates false dispatches. When your monitoring center can visually confirm whether an alarm is real before calling police, they simply do not dispatch for false activations. No dispatch, no violation, no fine. Businesses that switch to verified monitoring consistently report dramatic reductions in false alarm fines within the first year.

What a Well-Designed Video Verification System Looks Like

Effective video verification is not just about having cameras — it is about having the right cameras in the right places, integrated with an alarm system and monitoring platform built for verified response. Here is what to look for:

Camera placement aligned with alarm zones. Each alarm sensor — door contacts, motion detectors, glass break sensors — needs a corresponding camera view that captures the activated area with sufficient resolution to identify individuals and confirm activity. A camera pointed at the wrong angle or with a blind spot over the primary entry point defeats the purpose.

Sufficient resolution and low-light performance. Most break-ins happen in darkness or low-light conditions. Your cameras need adequate resolution and infrared or low-light capability to provide the monitoring operator with a usable image. Low-resolution cameras that produce grainy nighttime images cannot support effective verification.

Reliable local storage with fast clip retrieval. Monitoring centers need to pull video quickly when an alarm triggers. Systems that rely on slow cloud uploads or have unreliable connectivity create delays that undermine the verification process. Local NVR storage with redundant backup is the right architecture.

Integration between alarm panel and camera system. True video verification requires tight integration between your alarm panel and your camera platform. When a sensor triggers, the system needs to automatically surface the relevant camera feed to the monitoring center without operator intervention to find it manually.

A monitoring center with video verification capability. Not all central monitoring stations support video verification. Choosing the right monitoring partner is as important as the hardware. You need a UL-Listed monitoring center with trained video operators and the platform infrastructure to support verified dispatch.

The Business Types That Benefit Most

Video verification delivers value across virtually every commercial property type, but the impact is especially significant for:

  • Retail businesses — high-value inventory, frequent after-hours delivery activity, and multiple entry points create both high false alarm risk and high actual theft risk
  • Restaurants and bars — late-night environments with cash on premises and staff who may accidentally trigger alarms during closing
  • Warehouses and distribution facilities — large footprints, valuable goods, and shift-based operations that create frequent after-hours activity
  • Medical and dental offices — controlled substances, expensive equipment, and sensitive patient data create high-value targets
  • Auto dealerships and service facilities — vehicles, parts inventory, and tool theft are consistent targets in the Chicago metro area
  • Any business that has received false alarm fines — if you have already been cited, the ROI on video verification is immediate
